# Internet section: fresh visual review, 24 October 2026

## Judgment

The section has useful analysis, especially Figures 10–12, but its visual communication is uneven. The main problems are dense country marks without a clear focal comparison, repeated full-browser screenshots, small text, and several successive summaries that demand more explanation than their visible evidence supplies. A larger number of exotic charts will not solve this.

Reviewed: the three-page assignment brief, current REPORT.md and all 16 internet figure assets, selected original images at larger size, relevant September 22/24/29 lecture passages, existing research trials, and new external references below. The current PDF has 27 pages. Older research notes contain outdated page counts and should not be treated as the current deliverable specification.

## Assignment constraints

The brief requires three interactive network layouts; a JavaScript bundled parallel-coordinate plot with brushing and/or axis reordering; JavaScript zoomable treemaps with different table-to-tree remodelings; and at least two variants each of horizon and ridgeline plots. Additional heatmaps or trajectory plots cannot replace these requirements. Correctness and quality of inferences account for 8 points each, data handling for 10, completeness for 4. The brief specifies no target figure count.

## Two new previews using the existing data

1. **Country-by-year adoption atlas** — [PNG](../output/internet_review_20261024/adoption_atlas.png), [SVG](../output/internet_review_20261024/adoption_atlas.svg). All 159 complete-panel countries over 21 years; rows grouped by supplied region then sorted by 2020 adoption; shared 0–100% sequential color scale and printed endpoints. This supplies the country identities and historical overview that regional medians hide. Best as a full-page or zoomable companion, not squeezed into a report column. It complements the required horizons. Ordering by endpoint is a display choice, not evidence of natural clusters.

2. **Adoption–broadband trajectories** — [PNG](../output/internet_review_20261024/adoption_broadband_paths.png), [SVG](../output/internet_review_20261024/adoption_broadband_paths.svg). Six named illustrative cases, with all 11 observed annual pairs from 2010–2020 and identical axes. South Africa changes by +48.1 adoption percentage points and +0.7 fixed broadband subscriptions/100; China by +35.8 points and +24.6/100. This makes the contrast between national paths directly inspectable. Countries were purposefully chosen to illustrate contrasting paths, not sampled representatively. Fixed broadband subscriptions are not people; these paths neither prove mobile substitution nor identify causation.

The previews are separate from the submission. Original figures, report, browser apps and presentation are unchanged. Reproduce with `python -X utf8 scripts/preview_internet_review_20261024.py` from `ass2`. CSVs and validation.json accompany the outputs. Checks cover unique country-year keys, complete annual observations, 0–100 adoption bounds for the atlas, and cohort dimensions; no values were imputed. Both PNGs were visually inspected and label spacing revised.

## Recommended narrative

Open with **when and where adoption changed** (atlas), then **different infrastructure paths** (trajectories and a purposeful PCP brush), then **who resembles whom** (controlled network layouts and profiles), then **where online users are concentrated** (two treemap remodelings), and finish with **whether the divide narrowed** (inequality, regional gains, required horizons and ridgelines). Consolidate required comparisons into legible multi-panel figure sets instead of increasing the figure count indiscriminately.

If only three upgrades are feasible: improve PCP selection evidence; make the treemap comparison use identical root support; and add the trajectory small multiples. The atlas is valuable at large size but is not the best compact report figure.

## Research and attribution

- **Course, September 22, pp. 27–29:** axis reordering, correlation patterns, edge bundling, distributions on PCP axes. **September 24, pp. 4, 6–7:** brushing, cross-filtering, and data remodeling. **September 29, pp. 6–7, 9, 11, 14–15, 18:** horizon construction, distributions, and small multiples. Read directly from local lecture PDFs; use these to justify improvements to the required techniques.
- [Syntagmatic's original parallel-coordinate examples](https://syntagmatic.github.io/parallel-coordinates/): primary implementation examples of brushing, reordering, bundling and a linked data table; relevant to showing a meaningful selected subset. This is a design reference, not a recommendation to adopt an old library version.
- [D3 treemap documentation](https://d3js.org/d3-hierarchy/treemap) and [Mike Bostock's zoomable treemap](https://observablehq.com/@d3/zoomable-treemap): primary references for hierarchical layout and drilldown. Different tiling and different data hierarchy are separate experiments.
- [Heer, Kong and Agrawala, Sizing the Horizon](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/horizon): primary research on chart height, layering and comparison accuracy; supports careful sizing and explaining the encoding rather than assuming compactness guarantees readability.
- [Chimdi Nwosu, Internet Access Across the World](https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/showcase/7261-internet-access-across-the-world-2003-to-2022): public project description of sorted country tiles, regional/income grouping and a time-series view. Useful internet-specific visual inspiration. Its data interval and income grouping were not imported into our data, and the linked live Tableau dashboard was not tested.
- [Zlatan B, A short guide to temporal heat maps, Medium](https://medium.com/@spectalizer/a-short-guide-to-temporal-heat-maps-eef64deee0a1): accessible article discussing temporal grids. Its example is a date-by-hour electricity heatmap. Our country-by-year matrix is an adaptation of the dense rectangular overview idea, not its calendar design or a claim of subannual internet measurements.
- [Darío Weitz, Parallel Coordinates Plots, Medium](https://medium.com/data-science/parallel-coordinates-plots-6fcfa066dcb3): only the public introduction was accessible; member-only body was not used. Useful discovery/context; implementation recommendations above use the lecture and primary examples.
- [From Data to Viz: connected scatterplot](https://www.data-to-viz.com/graph/connectedscatter.html) and [heatmap](https://www.data-to-viz.com/graph/heatmap.html): chart-form references. Time order must be explicit in connected scatterplots; shared scales matter when comparing paths. No statistical inference is derived from these design pages.

## Designs not prioritized

Do not add radar charts, decorative chord diagrams, 3D scatterplots or a large Sankey simply to look advanced. They would either obscure positional comparisons or require another transformation with little added evidence. A geographic map can answer a spatial question, but its large-country area emphasis is less suitable for comparing equally weighted national trajectories. The previous inequality and raincloud work already addresses distributions well; further distribution variants should earn their place through a distinct question.
